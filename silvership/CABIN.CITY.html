Silver's Private Quarters
"Every man has a room where he keeps the truth about himself. For most men, that room is locked. For Silver, the room is on a stolen ship between the stars, and the lock is a lie he told so long ago he forgot it was a lie."
📜

THE CAPTAIN'S DESK

▼
"The desk is where Silver sits alone. Not the Silver who commands, not the Silver who charms, not the Silver who kills. The Silver who calculates. The rum is for the other Silvers. The desk gets the numbers."

A massive wooden desk bolted to the metal floor — the only thing on this ship that doesn't move. Star charts layered on star charts. A brass sextant modified with holographic displays that project trade routes into the air above. Half-empty rum bottles serve as paperweights. Scattered gold coins from fourteen systems. The leather captain's chair has two worn armrests — only one gets used.

Behind the desk, the stern window frames deep space like a painting that changes every hour. Silver chose this cabin for that window. He chose everything for a reason. The parrot perches on a brass stand to his left. It repeats coordinates in its sleep. Captain Flint — named after the only man Silver ever feared. Now a bird. Demoted.

THE SLEEPING QUARTERS

▼
"One boot on the floor. One wooden leg leaning against the wall. The pistol is loaded. The Bible is open. The locket is closed. This is everything you need to know about the man."

A hammock — not a bed. Silver trusts the sway. Born on ships, dies on ships. The hammock is strung between two carved wooden posts bolted to metal walls. Thick wool blankets. A fur throw stolen from a governor's mansion on Proxima-6.

The nightstand tells the truth the man won't: a pistol (loaded, always), a Bible (open to Ecclesiastes — vanity, vanity), a bottle (half full — Silver is an optimist), and a locket with a woman's portrait. He's never said her name. The crew has never asked. At the foot of the hammock: a sea chest, padlocked. Even Silver doesn't open it often. Some treasure is too personal to count.

THE WAR TABLE

▼
"Silver doesn't plan battles. He plans the conversation after the battle, where the winner gives him what he wanted before the first shot was fired."

A round wooden table with a brass rim. The holographic projector in the center was ripped from a naval frigate. It projects star systems in ghost-blue light — planets spinning, asteroid belts crawling, trade routes pulsing in orange. And there — on a jungle moon in the Flint system — a glowing X. The treasure.

Silver's crutch leans against the table like a seventh crew member. Chairs pushed back — the meeting just ended. Empty rum mugs. A knife stabbed through a contract on the table surface — someone disagreed. They don't disagree anymore. On the walls: mounted alien skulls and crossed cutlasses. The cabin of a man who wins arguments by having them last.

THE TROPHY WALL

▼
"Other men keep trophies to remember what they've done. Silver keeps trophies to remind other men what he's capable of."

The dark wooden wall behind his desk. Every item mounted, spotlit, curated. This is not decoration. This is a curriculum vitae written in stolen objects.

An alien crystal skull from the Caves of Cygnus. A golden compass that points to nowhere — because "nowhere" is where the treasure is. Crossed plasma cutlasses — his and Flint's, from the last time they disagreed. A framed Jolly Roger — the original, from the Walrus. A jeweled crown from a conquered alien king who begged. A collection of keys — one from every ship Silver ever took. Seventeen keys. Seventeen captains who lost everything.

THE CAPTAIN'S WINDOW

▼
"The most beautiful view on the ship, and only one man sees it. This is what power looks like. Not the gold. Not the guns. The window."

The stern window. A massive curved glass spanning the entire back wall. Through it: the universe. Tonight it's a nebula — deep oranges and blues boiling in slow motion, stars scattered through the gas like diamonds dropped in honey.

Inside: a worn leather armchair facing the glass. A small table with a glass of rum catching the nebula light — the rum turns orange, then blue, then orange again as the ship rotates. A pipe still smoking. Silver sits here after the crew sleeps. After the plans are made. After the rum is poured. He sits here and looks at the universe he's been stealing from, and the universe looks back, and neither of them blinks.

🔐

THE SECRET COMPARTMENT

▼
"Every man keeps a secret. Silver keeps a secret behind a secret behind a wooden panel behind a lie. And inside that secret is a portrait of a boy he didn't kill when he should have. That's the most dangerous thing on this ship."

Behind the third wooden panel on the port wall — the one that looks exactly like every other panel — a hidden vault carved into the hull. You press the knot in the wood. The panel swings. Inside:

The REAL map. Not the one on the war table. Not the one the crew has seen. A glowing alien crystal tablet showing coordinates that shift like living things. Stacks of alien gold bars. A velvet pouch of uncut gems that catch the light and throw it back changed. A personal journal bound in black leather — every page in Silver's hand, every entry a confession no priest will ever hear.

And at the back, small, in a simple frame: a portrait of a boy. Jim Hawkins. The one Silver let live. The one Silver let go. The most expensive thing in the vault — because it cost Silver something he didn't know he had.

THE CABIN RULES

▼
"Silver's cabin has three rules. Break one and you swim home."

RULE ONE: Knock. Wait. If you hear "Enter," enter. If you hear nothing, walk away. If you hear the parrot laugh, run.

RULE TWO: Don't touch the desk. Don't touch the locket. Don't touch the third panel on the port wall. Don't ask about the third panel on the port wall.

RULE THREE: The rum on the right side of the desk is for guests. The rum on the left side is Silver's. If you can't tell left from right, you have bigger problems than rum.

"The cabin is the only honest place on a dishonest ship. Silver lies to every man alive. But in this room, alone, with the stars and the rum and the locket, he lies only to himself. And even then — not always."
