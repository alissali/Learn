The Training Room

Where the crew becomes dangerous

I. The Combat Arena

Deck 4, Section 7 — Main Training Hall

The largest open space on the ship that is not the cargo hold. Padded metal floors scarred from a thousand sword strikes. Weapon racks along every wall — cutlasses next to energy blades, boarding pikes next to stun batons. The training dummies are stuffed spacesuits. Some of them have names.

Training Log — Day 4,291: "New recruit lasted eleven seconds against the dummy. The dummy is not programmed to fight back. Reassigned to kitchen duty."

II. The Zero-G Chamber

Deck 4, Section 9 — Spherical Module

A spherical room where gravity dies on command. Magnetic grip panels on every surface. The crew trains to fight floating — because boarding actions happen in zero-G and the ones who panic are the ones who die. Holographic enemies flicker blue. They do not feel pain. The crew does.

Training Log — Day 3,847: "Simulated boarding action. Crew performed adequately. Jenkins floated into a wall at speed and is now afraid of the color blue. Progress."

III. The Shooting Range

Deck 5, Section 2 — Starboard Corridor

A long narrow corridor with blast walls at the far end, cratered and scorched from a decade of plasma fire. Old flintlock pistols hang next to energy weapons on the rack. The paper targets are shaped like naval officers. Nobody questions this. Ammunition is rationed. Accuracy is not optional.

Training Log — Day 4,103: "Ammunition audit. 847 rounds fired this week. 12 hit the target. Silver ordered the range extended by ten meters. Morale has not improved."

IV. The Ring

Deck 3, Cargo Bay 2 — Converted

A fighting ring built from cargo crates and thick rope in a low-ceilinged bay. Bloodstains on the canvas that nobody cleans because they are the scoreboard. A punching bag made from a stuffed duffel bag swings from chains. Crew initials carved into the corner posts. Friday nights, the whole ship comes down. Silver watches from the doorway. He never bets. He already knows.

Training Log — Day 4,200: "Friday bout: Cookie vs. the new navigator. Navigator has reach. Cookie has rage. Cookie won in forty seconds. The navigator can still navigate. Barely."

V. The Navigation School

Deck 2, Section 5 — Forward Observatory Annex

The quiet room. A massive holographic star map projects from the center table. Young crew members study charts, plot courses, learn to read solar winds. Old paper maps pinned to the walls next to holographic displays. A chalkboard covered in equations and hand-drawn constellations. Brass telescopes sit next to quantum sensors. Silver insists every pirate learn to navigate. A pirate who cannot find his way home is not a pirate. He is cargo.

Training Log — Day 4,155: "Navigation exam. Three passed. Two failed. One drew a picture of a cat on the star chart. The cat was accurate. He passed."

VI. The Medical Bay

Deck 2, Section 8 — Port Side

Half butcher shop, half surgery. An old wooden table with leather straps sits next to a modern med-pod. Anatomical charts of human and alien bodies cover the walls. Jars of specimens line the shelves — nobody asks what is in them. Rusty 18th century bone saws hang next to laser scalpels. The practice dummy has removable limbs. It has been reassembled wrong so many times it now has three arms and the crew calls it Lucky.

Training Log — Day 4,089: "Field surgery drill. Patient survival rate: 60%. This is an improvement. The 40% would disagree but they are practice dummies and their opinion is not recorded."

Training Room Rules

  1. The Arena is open 0600-2200. After 2200, injuries are your problem.
  2. No live ammunition in the Ring. Last time cost us a bulkhead.
  3. Zero-G sessions require a spotter. Jenkins is not a valid spotter.
  4. Navigation students must pass the exam or scrub the engine room. Both teach you where you are.
  5. The medical dummy is named Lucky. Respect Lucky.
  6. Silver trains alone at 0500. Do not enter. Do not watch. Do not ask what he trains for.
